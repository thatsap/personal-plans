## WHO I AM

My name is **Ashutosh Pandey**.

I'm 23 years old. I live in India — same city throughout my life. I live in a **rented room**. I wasn't handed anything. I come from a **lower middle class family** where living is not made easily.

I'm a **mechanical engineering graduate** who pivoted into software because I saw where the world was going and I seized it.

I build things that matter to me:
- **Three.js** and **WebGL** applications
- **3D configurators** and **digital twins**
- Full-stack client work — B2B CRUD portals, manufacturing-adjacent systems
- **Backend-as-a-Service**, system design

My domain interest is **Indian mid-market manufacturing** — productized configurators over one-off builds. I don't want to rescue failing projects for free. I want to build a white-label configurator template in 2–3 weeks with setup fee + SaaS retainer.

I'm a **vegetarian**. I take supplements. I don't smoke. I don't drink. I channel stress through work, gym, and discipline — not substances. I'm what I'd call a rare gentleman, even if I don't feel like one right now.

I play **chess** — I log games and drill weaknesses. I play **guitar** — depth over breadth, one song done right. I'm mostly **silent** — about 80% of my day. Small circle. I'm working on not over-sharing energy with close colleagues at office.

I sleep about **6 hours a night**. I report it as adequate, though I've cut sleep before for worse reasons.

I'm **virgin**. Part of my mental loop ties to unlived experience regret. I don't primarily use lust as escape — I often imagine her even when triggered by unrelated stimuli.

I have a personal project in this repo called **Time-Manager** — productivity and time discipline tooling aligned with how I want to live: structure days so they compound, reduce open loops, support my morning technical block and "today's three outputs."

## WHERE I COME FROM

I was handed **nothing**.

Not metaphorically. Literally.

I come from a lower middle class family in India. My parents did what they could, but there was no inherited financial advantage. No safety net. No connections that opened doors. No room to fail comfortably.

Living was not made easy for us. Every rupee had weight. Every decision had cost.

I went to a **simple government college**. The yearly fees were **₹1500**. Not a typo. Fifteen hundred rupees per year.

That was my college.

---

## COLLEGE — SURVIVAL MODE

College was not a coming-of-age experience for me. It was **survival**.

### The Room

I lived in a **single room with a toilet**. That's it. A square, small room and a toilet. Nothing else.

I **shared that room with 4 people**.

Four of us. One room. One toilet.

### The Money

My daily budget for eating was around **₹100**.

Some days, if I ate slightly more, I would have to **starve myself the next day** to balance it out.

There was a day I had only **₹11** with me.

On that day, I drank **buttermilk** and slept.

That's not a story I tell for sympathy. That's just what happened.

### The Full Cost

My whole college — **4 years** — including:
- Traveling fees
- Accommodation
- Books
- Internet
- Everything

...was done under **₹2 lakh**.

I didn't just attend college. I **survived** it.

---

## THE CAMPUS INTERVIEW AND THE CLIMB

I got into the **campus interview**.

The field was **completely new** for me. I had no one guiding me. No mentor in my pocket. No family member who'd walked this path before.

So I **worked my ass off day and night**.

Every day.

I was learning in real time while everyone else seemed to have a map. I didn't have a map. I had **work**.

I started my job at **20**.

By **22**, I became **tech lead**.

Two years. From nothing to tech lead.

While still carrying everything else.

---

## BOTTLING EVERYTHING UP

Throughout college and the early career climb, I had **no one to speak to**.

Not really.

I bottled up everything inside. Never let it out. That wasn't a strategy — it was the only thing I knew. You survive. You carry. You don't burden. You don't crack.

I became very good at functioning while bleeding internally.

I didn't know yet that this would make what came next hit me ten times harder.

---

## SHE CAME

Then she came into my life.

At first, I didn't share anything with her. I didn't know how. I'd spent years not sharing.

Then, slowly, slowly — I did.

**For the first time in my life**, I found someone whom I could share my things with.

The room with four people.
The ₹11 day.
The loneliness.
The pressure.
The climb.
All of it — I started letting her see it.

She wasn't just a girlfriend.

She was the **first person who felt like home**.

One thing led to another. We got closer. I opened more. I gave more.

And I thought — finally. Finally, I don't have to carry everything alone.

---

## HOW I CHANGED FOR HER

She started becoming **demanding**. I agreed.

She asked me to **change**. I changed.

I changed myself so much that **I don't remember what I was before**.

I adored her.

She kept feeling **superior** to me — and I let it be that way because I thought that's what love was. I thought love meant accommodating. Adjusting. Accepting.

I thought if I just gave enough, changed enough, showed up enough — it would be enough.

I was wrong.

---

## WHAT I WAS BUILDING WHILE LOVING HER

While all of this was happening, I wasn't standing still. I was **building**:

- I got **promotions**
- I started my job at **20**, became **tech lead at 22**
- I **provided for my family**
- I **paid my sister's fees**
- I **taught her every day**
- I started **studying for government exams**
- I dealt with **kidney stones** during this time — and kept going

I was solving things. Going deeper into my work. Becoming more capable. Becoming more valuable.

Not because I didn't love her. Because **this is who I am**. I build. I climb. I carry.

Even when it hurts. Even when I'm tired. Even when I'm alone in a rented room at 1:35 AM wondering why I'm not enough.

---

## WHAT SHE NEEDED VS WHAT I WAS BECOMING

She didn't like me going deeper into work.

She wanted **an hour or two every day** with her — on call, on chat, present — or she would get angry.

So I **cut my sleep** to talk to her. That's how much I cared.

I would finish work exhausted, run on fumes, and still show up for her because I thought that's what you do for someone you love.

I tried my best.

I gave hours I didn't have.
I gave versions of myself I couldn't sustain.
I gave silence and softness and presence even when my body was asking for rest.

**Yet I was not enough.**

I was becoming tech lead at 22 while paying my sister's fees and fighting kidney stones — and I was still not enough.

We wanted different lives.

She wanted a boyfriend who was always available, always adjustable, always orbiting her.

I was trying to become a man who could lift his entire family out of where we started.

Those two things were going to collide.

They did.

---

## THE END

One fine day, she told me:

**"You made me bitter."**

And that was the end.

Not a conversation. Not a slow unwinding. Not mutual closure.

A verdict.

*You made me bitter.*

After everything I gave. After every change I made. After every hour of sleep I cut. After every piece of myself I bent to keep her.

I made her bitter.

I believed it.

I still believe it sometimes — at 1:39 AM when the urge to text her rises and I think maybe if I just explain myself, if I just have five minutes, if I just apologize — maybe she'd see.

But she won't.

Because the story she told herself at the end wasn't about everything I carried. It was about how I made her feel.

And I wasn't there to defend myself. I just absorbed it.

---

## AFTERMATH — WHAT IT DID TO ME

Now she's **happy with someone else**.

Posting stories with other men.
Changing her profile picture.
Roaming. Enjoying.
Probably has a new boyfriend.

And I'm in my rented room.

### The Rot

The breakup didn't just make me sad. It created **rot** — the main drag on my engineering velocity. Not skill. Not schedule. **This.**

- **Intrusive memories** — multiple times a day, 5–10+
- **Rumination** — compulsive mental loops
- **Sexual fantasies** tied to her, even when triggered by unrelated stimuli
- Urge to **text her**, apologize, beg for one more chance
- Checking her **stories** and feeling the knife twist of asymmetry — she's fine, I'm not
- The fear that **she'll have dozens of men like me or better, but I won't find anyone like her**
- The **unlived experience** layer — virginity, regret, "what could have been"
- Feeling like a **fucking loser** at 1:35 AM after surviving ₹11 buttermilk days and becoming tech lead at 22

I've tried:
- Digital detox
- Cold exposure
- Pattern interrupts
- Scheduled grief windows
- Axiom and mantra frameworks (Kaal, Arjuna, Vergil, Vector)

Some of it helps. Physical interventions help. Frameworks help in spikes.

**But as soon as I'm away from flow — it comes back.**

When I'm playing badminton, doing cardio, intensely coding — I'm happy. I'm clear. I don't have this issue.

The moment I'm idle — after work, lying in bed, transitions, gaps — **every fucking time**, the loop returns.

### What I Feel

- My heart aches to text her
- I feel overwhelmed — now even while working
- I feel like I lost **intent** — the thing that made me focus and avoid everything
- I feel like a machine when I'm in protocol mode and human when I'm breaking
- I said I never want to love again
- I feel like I lost her forever
- I feel like I won't get anyone like her again
- I feel like she won and I lost

## JULY 13, 2026 — THE DAY I BURNED THE SHRINE

On July 13, 2026, I made the hardest move.

I **deactivated my Instagram account**.

I **deleted her contact**.

I **deleted all messages**.

I **deleted everything I ever created for her** — websites, songs, poetries. All of it.

Everything I ever had of her. **Gone.**

I sat in the silence afterward and felt like I lost her forever. Like everything was just gone.

The emptiness wasn't peace. It was **withdrawal**.

But I did it because some part of me knows: staying connected was killing my velocity.

### My Rules Going Forward

- **Instagram reactivation:** Only at **12% body fat**, to post — not to check on her
- Must also pass: **90 days no contact**, indifference test, mute/block her before posting
- **48-hour rule:** Don't undo any deletion for 48 hours when the urge hits
- **No texting.** Not for closure. Not for five minutes. Not at 1:39 AM.
