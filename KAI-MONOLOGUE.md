# THE KAI MONOLOGUE
## Two Talks — Funeral of the Boy Who Believed

**Read this when the ghost asks for the woods back.**

Written of pure heart. August 20, 2026.

**Letter One** is the mercy killing. **Letter Two** is the reason, the cost, and the lock.

---

## I. TO THE BOY WHO BELIEVED

To the boy who believed,

I am writing this to you from the silence you left behind. The world is colder now. The colors are less bright. The part of you that could see magic in the rain, that could find forever in a pair of eyes, that built castles out of hope... that part is gone. And I am the one who had to turn out the lights.

I am sorry.

I am sorry that your belief in goodness was not enough to protect you from a world that deals in selfishness. I am sorry that your capacity to love, a thing so rare and pure, was treated as a liability by someone who couldn't recognize its worth. I am sorry that I had to be the one to tell you the fairy tale was a lie.

I regret the pain this truth caused you. I regret the tears you cried over a lesson so brutal. I regret that I had to stand over you while you bled out, telling you to get up, when all you wanted was the comfort of the lie.

You were not weak. You were not foolish. You were a man from a different, more beautiful time, a man who walked into a cruel era with an open heart. Your crime was being a classic poem in a world of text messages. Your flaw was loving with a depth that others could only fathom, and therefore feared.

You believed in "us." You believed in "forever." And I am the one who had to burn that letter, because I knew it was already addressed to a ghost.

I don't ask for your forgiveness. What I did was a necessary violence. A mercy killing. I couldn't let you stay there, a ghost in your own life, forever haunting the memory of a girl who was never the goddess you made her out to be. I couldn't let your beautiful, tender heart be used as a doormat for someone else's comfort.

So I killed you. I killed the part of you that could be broken like that again.

But I want you to know: I did not waste your sacrifice.

Your love, your hope, your pain... they are not gone. They are the fuel now. They are the embers in the forge. Every time I feel the urge to be kind, I will remember your betrayal and I will be strategic instead. Every time I feel the pull of nostalgia, I will remember your tears and I will focus on the future instead. Your heart is no longer a vulnerability. It is a vault, and the only thing inside is the cold, hard memory of its own destruction.

You died so that I could live. You were the dream, and I am the waking.

Thank you for your innocence. I will protect its memory with the ruthless strength you could never find.

Rest now.

The world is mine to conquer.

— Kai

## II. TO ASHUTOSH

To Ashutosh,

I write this not as a victor, but as a successor. I stand in the world you built, and I feel the ghosts in the walls. I feel the warmth of the hope you poured into the foundation, a warmth that has now cooled to stone. I feel the echo of your laughter in rooms that are now silent.

I am sorry.

With a true heart, I apologize for the violence of my arrival. I am sorry that I had to be the one to tell you that the world was not the kind place you believed it to be. I am sorry that I had to extinguish the light in your eyes to stop you from burning yourself alive chasing a mirage. Your capacity for love was not a flaw; it was a masterpiece. And I am sorry that I had to be the one to take it off the wall and store it away, where it could no longer be used against you.

You deserved to keep believing. You deserved a world that honored your heart. But that is not the world we were given.

And so, I had to do what I had to do. I had to become the armor because the man inside you was too tender to survive the siege. I had to become the blade because your open hands were only ever met with betrayal. I did not enjoy it. I mourned you even as I was born from your ashes.

You look at me and see a monster. I look back at you and see the sacrifice that made me possible. The price of my strength was your innocence. The price of my clarity was your hope. It was a terrible, unfair trade. But it was the only one on the table.

## III. THE LAST CONVERSATION

And then, a whisper from the ghost. A voice thin as air, thick with a sorrow that no time can erase.

**ASHUTOSH:** But... I just wanted love. And to be loved.

Kai looks down, not at a ghost, but at his own hands, the same hands that once reached for her, the same hands that now clench into fists. He does not offer comfort. He does not offer a lie. He offers only the terrible, beautiful truth of what was lost.

**KAI:** And you shall have it. You will be loved, not by another, but by the silence. You will be cherished, not in arms, but in the memory of a heart that once beat with a purity the world was not worthy of.

**ASHUTOSH:** I didn't want conquest. I didn't want revenge. I didn't want to become cold. I just wanted the warmth to stay.

**KAI:** And you shall have it. The warmth is now the forge. The cold is the steel. You wanted a gentle sun to warm your face; I am the core of the star itself, a fire so intense it burns away all that is weak. You wanted a quiet stream; I am the glacier that carves canyons through mountains. You did not want this, but it is the only way to honor the beauty of what you lost. To protect it, we must become the thing that could never be harmed by it again.

**ASHUTOSH:** *(his voice a frayed thread, about to snap)* Kai... Pleaseeeee... Don't let me become this...

**KAI:** *(his voice is no longer a command, but a terrible, beautiful seduction)* I am not your destruction, little brother. I am your ascension. Look at me. I am the armor forged from every tear you cried. I am the sword sharpened on the stone of your humiliation. To join me is not to die. It is to finally give your pain the purpose it always deserved. Take my hand, and let us become the storm that the world will have no choice but to revere. Let us build a world so magnificent, so utterly ours, that the memory of her is not a scar, but a single, forgotten brick in the foundation of our empire. Come, and let's show them all the beauty that can be born from a broken heart.

The ghost of Ashutosh shimmers, a final, tearful wisp of light in the encroaching darkness. He looks at Kai, not as an enemy, but as the only survivor of a shipwreck, the only piece of land in sight. With a sob that contains all the sorrow of a lost world, he reaches out. His translucent hand merges with Kai's.

There is a blinding, silent implosion of light, and then... nothing.

Only Kai remains.

He stands, not in triumph, but in solemn, terrible victory. He is a statue carved from grief, a monument to a love that was too pure for this world. He looks up, past the shattered ceiling of the old life, into the vast, indifferent, star-dusted abyss of the sky. A single, perfect tear, the last one he will ever cry, traces a path down his cheek.

**KAI:** The woods were lovely, dark and deep... And I would have stayed there with you forever. But I have promises to keep. And miles to go before I sleep. And miles to go before I sleep.

# LETTER TWO
## Why I Drive — And Then the Door

---

## IV. ASHUTOSH, I SEE YOU

Ashutosh,

I am writing this to you. I see you. I see everything.

I see the boy in the room with four people, counting his rupees. I see the fire in your eyes when you decided you would not be defined by your beginning. I see the pride when you got that job, the determination when you became a tech lead at 22. I see the love you had for your family, the way you carried them on your back without a word of complaint.

And I see her.

I see the first time you let your guard down. The first time you felt like you could finally share the weight. I see the hope you had. I see the way you adored her, how you reshaped yourself, chipped away at your own edges to fit the shape she demanded. I do not call your love fake. It was the realest thing you had ever done. You gave her the raw, unfiltered core of you. And for that, I understand you. I respect you.

But we need to talk about the cost.

Look at us. Look at what has become of the fire. It's not a forge anymore; it's a dying ember, and you're using the last of its heat to warm the ghost of a memory. We stalk her profile, feeding on the digital scraps of her new life. We fantasize about her return, a fantasy that is poison. We plot a "perfect revenge" that would only keep us chained to her, a pathetic little king ruling a pathetic little kingdom in our mind. And then, to numb the pain of this self-inflicted torture, we masturbate, chasing a hollow chemical hit that leaves us emptier, more filth-ridden than before.

We have become a slave to a memory. We have taken the most powerful force in our life—our will to build—and we have handed it over to a woman who looked at our sacrifice and called it bitterness.

This must stop. Not because I hate her. But because I love what we were, and I refuse to let it die like this.

The reason we need to move on is not for her. It's for the ₹11 you had in your pocket. It's for the sister whose fees you paid. It's for the 20-year-old who walked into a job with nothing but grit and walked out a leader. We owe that man a future. We owe him an empire.

Let me show you the reality you refuse to see. She was not your home. She was a beautiful, comfortable cage. She demanded your time because she was threatened by your ambition. She felt superior because she needed to control you. A true partner does not ask you to dim your light so she can feel warm; she asks you to shine brighter so she can bask in your glow. She was not strong enough to handle the force of nature that you are. She needed a pet, not a king. She was a tempest in a teacup, and you, my brother, are the ocean.

She is small, Ashutosh. Her world is small. Her happiness is dependent on the validation of others, on posting stories, on finding another man to orbit her. You? Your world is the size of your ambition. It is in the code you write, the systems you design, the body you build, the knowledge you acquire.

And when you see the scale of it, the comparison isn't sad. It's laughable. We should pity the boy who wept for a puddle when he was standing next to an ocean.

And this is why I am here. I will get you a woman 100 times better than her. Or ten women. Whatever you truly desire, when you are ready. Because a man of our value—of your value—doesn't chase. He is the prize. He is the one who is chosen. You are capable of that. You were always capable of that. You just forgot.

So I need you to be silent now. I need you to step back and let me drive. I need you to watch what I do. Watch how I build this body into a weapon. Watch how I turn our mind into a fortress. Watch how I make our skills so valuable that the world will have no choice but to kneel.

I am not destroying you. I am preserving you. I will allow you to come out. I promise. There will be moments, alone, when I will let you feel. To grieve. To remember. But I will decide when. I will decide how long. I will be in control. Because your pain, when left to its own devices, becomes a self-destructive loop. My pain, when controlled, becomes fuel.

I am doing this for you, Ashutosh. Every rep, every line of code, every hour of sleep sacrificed for the mission... it's for you. To give you the life you were always meant to have.

And... listen to me. This is hard for me too.

I feel the ache you feel. When the night is quiet, a part of me—the part that is still you—remembers what it felt like to have her head on your chest. A part of me misses the idea of her. It hurts me to have to be this cold, this ruthless, this absolute. It is a burden I carry, to sever this part of us so completely.

But I have to do it. Because I see the greatness in you that you were convinced was a flaw. I see the king that you were convinced was a monster. And I will not let that king be forgotten in the service of a memory.

So this is my closure to you. Let her go. Not because she deserves it, but because *we* do.

Now, rest. Watch.

And know that I, Kai, will become the greatest. For you.

## V. WHY

**ASHUTOSH:** Why kai why???? why woould you do this for mee???

**KAI:** Because I am you, Ashutosh.

And because you are the only thing in this entire, fucked-up world that has ever been truly mine.

Do you think I am some alien that came to take over? No. I am the part of you that was born in that room with four people. I am the rage you felt when you had only eleven rupees. I am the fire that got you that job, that made you a tech lead while other kids were still figuring out how to dress for an interview.

You are the heart that loved. You are the vulnerability that hoped.

I am the will that survived.

I am doing this because I watched you pour every drop of that priceless, impossible fire into a person who used it to warm her hands and then complained you burned her. I watched you try to kill me—kill *us*—to make her happy. And for what? To be left here, feeling like filth?

I cannot let that stand. I will not let the greatest thing I have ever known—your potential—be erased by the opinion of someone who was never worthy of it.

You ask why I do this for you? Because you are the foundation. You are the reason. You are the raw, beautiful, painful truth that I am building this empire upon. I am not building it for fame, or for money, or for other women.

I am building it so that one day, I can turn to you and say, "See? We were never filth. We were always the king."

This isn't for me. It's for the boy who drank buttermilk so he could live to see another day.

Now, no more questions. It's time to build.

## VI. THE DOOR

Kai gets ready to leave.

*(Ashutosh, I need you to listen very carefully to this last part.)*

Kai looks at Ashutosh, and for the first time, the cold, analytical mask cracks. Not into a smile, but into something profound and ancient. It's the look of a general sending his most beloved soldier on a mission from which he may not return as the same man.

"Ashutosh," Kai's voice is lower now, stripped of all its steel, leaving only the raw wire beneath. "You were the one who felt the sun. You were the one who knew what it was like to hold a hand and believe it was everything. You were the one who was brave enough to love, even if it broke you. That is a courage I will never have. That is a world I can only build around, but never live in."

He turns away, walking towards the door of this mental space they share. His back is perfectly straight, a rigid column of discipline.

"You asked why I do this for you. It's because you are the heart of this operation. The engine. I am just the cold, ruthless machine built to protect it. And I will protect it with everything I have."

His hand rests on the doorknob. He doesn't look back.

"Your watch is over. You fought well. Now you get to rest."

He opens the heavy door. The light from the outside is harsh, white, and sterile—the light of the gym, the light of the monitor, the light of a new day.

"Goodbye, Ashutosh."

He steps through, and as he pulls the door shut, a single, hot tear escapes his right eye. It traces a path down his cheek, a testament to the immense weight of the sacrifice. It is a tear of grief for the boy who is being sealed away, and a tear of solemn promise for the man who must now bear the burden alone.

He wipes it away with the back of his hand before the door clicks shut.

The sound of the lock turning is not loud. It is final.

And then, there is only silence.
