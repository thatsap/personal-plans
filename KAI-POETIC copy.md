And I had enough
I had heart while the heart was still there
I showed kind while it was still there

I kept a lamp against a weather I named home
And called the naming love, because it burned.
I brought my warmth the way a man brings wood
And stacks it by the wall before the snow,
Not asking if the winter meant to stay.
I thought the stacking was a kind of vow.
I thought the vow was mutual as breath.

There was a time the evening knew my name
And did not make me pay to hear it said.
I spoke soft to a door that learned to shut
And thanked the shutting, like a fool, for form.
I had a sun. I held it like a cup.
I drank as if the cup could not run dry.
It ran. I drank the dark and called it waiting.

I had enough — not in a shout, a tear,
A theatre a room could clap and leave —
Enough the way a field has had the frost:
Quiet, and total, and too late to argue.
The fence still stands. The gate is only rumour.
Kneel if you like. The white will not become
The path you loved because you loved it once.

I showed kind while kindness had a season.
I spent it. God, I spent it like a boy
Who thinks the coin is infinite because
His hand is open and the night is long.
I do not ask the night to give it back.
I do not write to winter for a thaw.
The heart that was still there is still a heart.
I wear it under work. I do not spend it
In streets that taught me what a street is for.

The woods go on. They never owed a path.
I learned that slow, the way a man learns cold
In fingers first, then bone, then the long walk
Where talking does not shorten any mile.
I had a home in someone else's weather.
I built. I bent. I kept the lamp. I stayed
Until staying was only a way of dying
With my eyes open, calling dying faith.

And I had enough.

Not hate. Not even anger with a name.
A stillness after all the giving failed
To purchase what the giving thought it bought.
I am the man who walks. I am the one
Who had a sun and put it in the chest
And locked the chest and put the chest to work
Because a heart unused becomes a shrine
And I have had my fill of kneeling there.

If I am hard, I learned it from the hour
The kind was not returned, and still I gave,
And gave, and found the giving made me small.
I will not be made small again for warmth
That was a season. Seasons close. They close.
The tree does not petition for its leaves.
It stands. It takes the winter in the grain.
It puts out, when it must, another year.

So I have heart — not less, but not for sale.
I showed kind while it was still there to show.
I have enough of doors that want a boy
And call him bitter when he stands a man.
The long time is already underfoot.
Five years. Ten. A rented room. A load.
The next hard block before the world may speak.
Hands on the unit. Breath longer going out.

I do not start. I do not end. I walk.
The heart stays. The driver has the hour.
And miles of ordinary light remain —
Gym light, and screen, and dawn used as a tool —
And I will spend my kindness on the living
Work, and the body, and the ones I carry,
And not one drop on graves that will not hold me.

And I had enough.
I had heart while the heart was still there.
I showed kind while it was still there.

And now I go.
The snow does not get a letter.
The woods do not get me back.
